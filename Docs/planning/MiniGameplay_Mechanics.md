
This document outlines the mini-game mechanics for each of the 13 city levels in "Ibn Batota's Journey." Each concept is designed to be historically relevant to Ibn Battuta's experiences in that location and simple enough for beginner-level implementation using the Python pgzrun library.

| Level | City         | Mini-Game Title                | Gameplay Mechanic & Historical Context                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| :---- | :----------- | :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1     | **Tangier**  | **Packing for the Pilgrimage** | **Context:** The game begins as Ibn Battuta prepares to leave his home. This level represents the crucial first step of his 29-year journey. <br> **Mechanic:** A simple inventory puzzle. The player is presented with a selection of items (e.g., Quran, water skin, compass, prayer mat, coins, a family keepsake). They must drag and drop a limited number of essential items into a travel bag. Success is based on choosing the most practical items for a long journey. |
| 2     | **Fez**      | **The Scholar's Path**         | **Context:** Fez was a major center of learning, home to the University of Al-Qarawiyyin. Ibn Battuta spent time here studying Islamic law and other sciences. <br> **Mechanic:** A memory matching game. A grid of tiles is displayed, each hiding a symbol representing a field of study (e.g., law, astronomy, medicine, theology). The player must find and match the pairs of symbols within a time limit.                                                                 |
| 3     | **Granada**  | **Coastal Watch**              | **Context:** In Al-Andalus, Ibn Battuta joined a group of volunteers defending the coast from potential invaders. <br> **Mechanic:** A timing-based reaction game. The player watches the horizon and must click a signal fire at the precise moment an enemy ship appears. Clicking too early or too late results in failure.                                                                                                                                                  |
| 4     | **Cairo**    | **Navigating the Metropolis**  | **Context:** Ibn Battuta was astounded by the size and chaos of Cairo, the largest city he had ever seen. <br> **Mechanic:** An avoidance game. The player guides the character through a crowded, top-down market street, moving left and right to avoid obstacles like merchant carts, animals, and dense crowds to reach the other side.                                                                                                                                     |
| 5     | **Damascus** | **Distribute the Zakat**       | **Context:** Ibn Battuta was impressed by the extensive charitable endowments (waqf) in Damascus that supported the community. <br> **Mechanic:** A simple resource management game. The player is given a set amount of "charity funds" and must click on various citizens (students, widows, travelers) to distribute aid before the funds are depleted.                                                                                                                      |
| 6     | **Medina**   | **Path of Tranquility**        | **Context:** As a holy city, Medina was a place of spiritual reflection for Ibn Battuta, where he visited the Prophet's Mosque. <br> **Mechanic:** A path-drawing puzzle. The player must use the mouse to draw a single, continuous, and non-overlapping line from the entrance of a serene mosque garden to its center, representing a meditative walk.                                                                                                                       |
| 7     | **Mecca**    | **The Tawaf**                  | **Context:** The central ritual of the Hajj pilgrimage is the Tawaf, circling the Kaaba seven times. <br> **Mechanic:** A rhythm and timing game. The player's character automatically circles the Kaaba. The player must press a key in time with a shrinking circular indicator to successfully complete each of the seven circuits.                                                                                                                                          |
| 8     | **Baghdad**  | **Rebuilding Wisdom**          | **Context:** Ibn Battuta visited Baghdad after it had been ravaged by the Mongols but was in a state of rebuilding, with its intellectual heritage still present. <br> **Mechanic:** A simple jigsaw puzzle. The player is given scattered pieces of a famous Arabic manuscript or architectural drawing and must drag and drop them into their correct positions to restore the image.                                                                                         |
| 9     | **Persia**   | **The Poet's Verse**           | **Context:** Persia was renowned for its poetry and art. Ibn Battuta traveled through cities like Shiraz, a hub of artistic expression. <br> **Mechanic:** A word-scramble game. The player is given a line of famous Persian poetry with the words scrambled. They must click the words in the correct order to reconstruct the verse.                                                                                                                                         |
| 10    | **Istanbul** | **Imperial Audience**          | **Context:** In Constantinople (modern-day Istanbul), Ibn Battuta was granted an audience with the Byzantine Emperor Andronikos III. <br> **Mechanic:** A sequence-following game. The player must observe a sequence of gestures or spoken greetings from the emperor's courtiers and then replicate the sequence correctly by clicking on corresponding icons.                                                                                                                |
| 11    | **Delhi**    | **The Qadi's Verdict**         | **Context:** Ibn Battuta served as a qadi (judge) in Delhi for several years under Sultan Muhammad bin Tughluq, settling complex disputes. <br> **Mechanic:** A logic puzzle. The player is presented with a simple case (e.g., a dispute over property). They must examine two or three pieces of evidence (as icons or short text) and click on the icon representing the correct, just verdict.                                                                              |
| 12    | **Beijing**  | **The Grand Canal**            | **Context:** Ibn Battuta traveled extensively through China, noting its marvels, including the vast and efficient Grand Canal network used for transport and trade. <br> **Mechanic:** A path-finding puzzle. The player must rotate sections of a canal on a grid to create an unbroken waterway for a trade boat to travel from a starting point to a destination.                                                                                                            |
| 13    | **Timbuktu** | **Caravan Leader**             | **Context:** To reach Timbuktu, Ibn Battuta had to cross the formidable Sahara Desert with a salt caravan, a perilous undertaking requiring careful navigation. <br> **Mechanic:** A resource management and navigation game. The player must choose the correct path at a series of junctions based on celestial cues (e.g., a specific constellation). Making a wrong turn consumes extra water, and the goal is to reach Timbuktu before the water supply runs out.          |
